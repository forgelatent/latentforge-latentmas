**Q1**
The single biggest way this rule could declare a **FAIL that is not real** is the clash between the strict leakage exclusion rule (Section 5) and the Founder's minimum sample-size requirement (E6), compounded by the proposed 41-event calibration split (Section 8). E6 defines "proven" as requiring at least 250 resolved markets on at least 150 independent events, while Section 8 allocates 41 of the 291 total events (E1, Section 2) to a calibration slice, leaving exactly 250 events for the confirmatory set. However, Section 5 requires excluding every market whose "forecast as of" date (first seen between March 23 and October 25, 2026, per E1) falls before the chosen model's training cutoff; if any open-weights checkpoint (or trained bridge per E4) has a cutoff after March 23, 2026, or if single-market events are dropped, the confirmatory pool immediately falls below the 250-event threshold in Section 8 (and threatens the 250-market / 150-event threshold in E6), while leaving too few independent events to statistically exclude a small positive margin $m$ once variance is accounted for.

**Q2**
**Drop the 41-event split and keep all qualifying events in the confirmatory set, estimating variance from the single-agent/base-rate arms or synthetic simulations instead.** The total pool has 291 events across 410 markets (E1, Section 2), and holding out 41 events leaves zero buffer above a 250-event confirmatory target before Section 5 leakage exclusions are even applied. Furthermore, Section 8 states the 41-event slice is used "only to estimate the spread of $d_e$, tuning nothing," which wastes ~14% of the statistical power on a variance estimate when E5 and E4 already show that shared-input latent effects are small (around +2.1 to ±2.8 points). If you only tune nothing on those 41 events, sacrificing sample size directly inflates the 95% bootstrap confidence interval width on the remaining events, making a real but small effect much harder to detect above $m$.

**Q3**
**Slices of one event must be scored independently** to stay consistent with Section 4 ("every arm... Does not get: ... any other market"). If you score slices as a set constrained to sum to 1 without showing the agent all sibling slices, you violate the agent's information set; if you do show all sibling slices, you violate Section 4 and alter the task for multi-slice events relative to single-market events. **Independent scoring can be gamed by a constant-low-probability bias:** because E1 and E2 show an 80.2% NO rate (329 NO vs 81 YES) driven by mutually exclusive event slices where only one slice wins, an arm whose channel induces conservative/under-confident probabilities (pushing estimates uniformly toward 0.15–0.20 regardless of content) will systematically achieve a much lower mean Brier score across multi-slice events than an arm that outputs well-reasoned 0.40–0.60 probabilities on individual questions in isolation.

**Q4**
**Insufficient.** Under Section 6 and E2 (where 80.2% of markets resolve NO), the deranged control **D** (injecting an unrelated question's latent states, Section 3) can degrade Agent B by pushing its predictions toward 0.50 (maximum entropy/confusion) or toward erratic high values, whereas **L** could simply preserve base-rate-like low outputs—meaning $\text{Brier}_D - \text{Brier}_L$ is strictly positive only because **D** actively harmed the receiver, not because **L** transmitted task-specific signal beyond what Agent B already had. **Required gate:** Require BOTH (1) the swap test $\text{mean}(\text{Brier}_D(e) - \text{Brier}_L(e)) > 0$ with the 95% bootstrap interval excluding zero, AND (2) the single-agent floor test $\text{mean}(\text{Brier}_S(e) - \text{Brier}_L(e)) > 0$ with the 95% bootstrap interval excluding zero (upgrading "L beats S" in Section 7 from a point-estimate comparison to a statistically bounded gate so prove-ably destructive or no-op relays cannot pass via **D** degradation alone).

**Optional**
**Section 7's definition of "Void" vs "Fail" makes the rule partially unable to fail on channel failure.** In Section 7, if the validity gate is not met ($\text{Brier}_D - \text{Brier}_L$ is not significantly above zero), the outcome is classified as **Void ("the test did not happen")** rather than **Fail**. However, E4 shows that in prior latent interfaces (LatentMAS, C2C), failing the swap test (0.60-point difference when swapping an unrelated message) *is* the central empirical failure mode of latent communication, and E6 lists "no latent method passes the swap test on this task" as an early-stop condition. Classifying a failed validity gate as "Void" instead of "Fail" allows a non-functioning latent channel to be written off as a non-test rather than a falsification of H3/H4 on that architecture. Additionally, in Section 7, "L beats both S and BR" does not specify whether that requires the 95% bootstrap interval to exclude zero or merely a raw mean difference greater than 0.
