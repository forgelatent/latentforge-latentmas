**Q5.**

1. **Series that repeat over time:** **One group if their active forecasting intervals (`first_seen` to `endDate` [E4]) overlap; separate groups only if the earlier event settles before the later event's `first_seen` date.** Under [E8], overlapping windows share the exact same underlying path and unobservables during the overlap, whereas strictly sequential, non-overlapping windows resolve on distinct realizations. However, if all markets in a recurring series share the exact same prompt template and base rate, keeping the whole `seriesSlug` as **one group** (as [E8] notes is the conservative call) is safer because model errors on a repeated template will be correlated across weeks even when the real-world windows do not overlap.
2. **Uneven group sizes and the 150-group floor:** If groups are very uneven in size (e.g., one mega-group of 40 events and 149 singletons), "at least 150 groups" [E2] **Still protects the error bars ONLY IF each outcome group receives equal weight (weight = 1 per group) in the primary endpoint and bootstrap, OR if the floor is stated in terms of Effective Sample Size ($N_{\text{eff}} = (\sum w_g)^2 / \sum w_g^2 \ge 150$).** Currently, [E6] says "uncertainty is computed by resampling groups," while the v0.1 rule weighted each *event* equally (weight 1 per event). If you resample groups of vastly unequal event counts while weighting by *event*, a handful of large groups will dominate the variance of the mean difference and cause the bootstrap distribution to be lumpy and under-powered; weighting each *outcome group* equally (so a group's score is the mean of its events' Brier scores, and each group counts once) prevents any large series from dominating the estimator.
