**Q3 — Agreement threshold.**

* **(i) How joined-pair agreement must be computed:** Computing overall agreement across all $\binom{291}{2} = 42,195$ event pairs would be massively inflated near 100% by true negatives (unrelated pairs). As specified in [E8] Step 3b ("agreement on *joined* pairs (pairs at least one reader joined)"), compute the **Jaccard index over event pairs**: let $P_A$ be the set of unordered event pairs $(i, j)$ placed in the same outcome group by Reader A (after taking transitive closure / connected components), and $P_B$ be the set of unordered event pairs placed in the same outcome group by Reader B. Then:

$$\text{Joined-Pair Agreement (Jaccard)} = \frac{\vert{}P_A \cap P_B\vert{}}{\vert{}P_A \cup P_B\vert{}}$$



Alongside overall Jaccard $J_{\text{all}}$, you must compute **non-mechanical Jaccard ($J_{\text{human}}$)** over the pairs where at least one reader deviated from the Step 2 mechanical candidate groups (i.e., pairs joined by at least one reader that were *not* linked in Step 2, unioned with Step 2 pairs that at least one reader split). Otherwise, if both readers passively accept 95% of Step 2 mechanical links [E8], $J_{\text{all}}$ will look high even if the readers completely disagree on every human judgment call.

* **(iii) What should happen below the threshold:** **Fail (trigger the early stop in [E1]/[E9]), NOT adjudicate anyway or add a third reader.** If two blind readers applying the exact same written definition [E8] to the exact same Step 1 packet cannot reach the pre-registered agreement threshold, the definition is empirically not reproducible on this dataset. Note also that in Step 3b, the rule "If the evidence does not settle it, merge" [E8] already guarantees that any surviving ambiguity *above* the threshold is resolved conservatively toward fewer groups (against a false PASS and toward the 150-group early-stop floor [E1, E9]).
