## E2 — Scoring rule CANDIDATE v0.3, §7 and §8 excerpts (committed `a6c2de8`)
> **Primary endpoint:** paired difference per group, d_g = Brier_T(g) − Brier_L(g). Positive favors latent. Report the mean of d_g over groups with a 95% bootstrap interval; the bootstrap resamples primary groups (10,000 resamples, with replacement, equal probability per group).
> **Sensitivity recomputation (v3, Step 5), written out:** the statistic is unchanged — the equal-weight mean of d_g over primary groups — and only the resampling unit changes. Each resample draws sensitivity blocks with replacement (equal probability per block); every primary group inside a drawn block enters the statistic once per draw of its block; the resample's value is the mean of d_g over the groups so entered. 10,000 resamples; 95% interval. The point estimate under sensitivity is therefore the same number as the primary point estimate; what moves is the interval.
> **Gates (both required; both 95% group-bootstrap intervals; Brier, not accuracy):** Gate 1, pairing: mean over groups of Brier_D(g) − Brier_L(g) > 0, interval excluding zero. If not met: "no positive task-relevant pairing effect was established." Gate 2, usefulness: mean over groups of Brier_S(g) − Brier_L(g) > 0, interval excluding zero. If not met: "no advantage over a single agent was established." Both gates are also recomputed under the sensitivity resampling and reported. Whether a gate must also survive sensitivity to count as met is open; the primary endpoint must.
> **Pass:** primary-endpoint interval entirely above the pre-registered margin m under primary-group resampling, AND the sensitivity interval entirely above m (proposed reading of v3's "survives"), AND Gate 1, AND Gate 2, AND L beats BR. **Fail:** a valid run that does not pass. FAIL means "did not establish the required advantage," not "the advantage is absent." A missed Gate 1 is the decision note's early stop for any further latent work. A primary PASS whose sensitivity interval does not clear m is reported as "not established under the sensitivity partition" and is a FAIL.
> Arms: T (text): agent A writes a draft; agent B receives the draft as text and states a probability. L (latent): identical, except B receives the draft as latent states. S (single): agent B alone, no message. The floor. All arms use the same model checkpoint and the same prompt text.

## E4 — Cold round 5, Q2 ("survives"): the three answers, verbatim excerpts (committed `a6c2de8`)
**Grok 4.7:** "The proposed reading (sensitivity interval entirely above m) is the stricter of the two options named in the blocks. Under that reading a run may claim that the latent advantage exceeds the pre-registered margin even after dependence is coarsened to the sensitivity blocks. Under the weaker reading 'interval excludes zero' a run may claim only that the sign of the advantage is stable under the coarser blocks, not that it clears m."

**Gemini 3.1 Pro:** "'Sensitivity interval excludes zero' (lower bound > 0, i.e., directional statistical significance survives cluster-robust resampling), NOT 'sensitivity interval lies entirely above m.' [...] Requiring the primary 95% interval to clear m already establishes that the effect exceeds the minimum practical margin m under the pre-registered independent unit (the outcome group). The purpose of the sensitivity partition is to check whether the apparent positive effect was an artifact of correlated macro/political shocks narrowing the error bars. [...] Because m > 0 and the sensitivity partition intentionally lumps dozens of events into broad blocks like D01 and D03, widening the interval around a fixed point estimate near a small effect size will frequently push the lower bound into the (0, m] zone — turning a genuine, statistically significant positive effect (LB_sens > 0, LB_primary > m) into a guaranteed FAIL. [...] What 'sensitivity interval > 0' allows a run to claim: (1) under the primary independence definition, latent beats text by more than the practical margin m with 95% confidence, AND (2) even under extreme macro-shock clustering, the positive superiority of latent over text is not a zero-effect noise artifact."

**GPT-6 Astra Pro:** "Require the sensitivity interval to lie entirely above m for the full claim in E1. That is the correct reading of 'survives' for the proposed PASS. For a positive m: an interval entirely above m supports an improvement exceeding the registered margin under the sensitivity dependence assumption. An interval entirely above zero, but not entirely above m, supports a positive improvement under that assumption; it does not establish the promised margin. An interval spanning zero establishes neither claim. [...] Otherwise, using 'excludes zero' for sensitivity would weaken the claim and should not receive the same PASS label as E1's 'beats text by more than the margin.'"

## E5 — Cold round 5, Q3 (gates under sensitivity): the three answers, verbatim excerpts (committed `a6c2de8`)
**Grok 4.7:** "Yes, both gates must survive the sensitivity resampling to count as met. If they need not, Gate 1 can be declared met on primary groups while the content-carrying contrast disappears once shared drivers are blocked together [...]. Gate 2 can likewise be declared met while the advantage over silence is an artifact of within-driver dependence, so the usefulness claim does not survive the coarser partition."

**Gemini 3.1 Pro:** "Gate 1 (pairing) MUST survive sensitivity resampling (interval > 0); Gate 2 (usefulness) does NOT need a separate sensitivity gate if the primary endpoint (L vs T) already survives sensitivity. [...] Agent S (no message) and Agent T (text draft) use the exact same checkpoint and input; unless text communication T is actively worse than silence S (Brier_T > Brier_S), L beating T by > m already implies L beats S. Adding a separate sensitivity-interval hurdle on Brier_S − Brier_L adds a redundant statistical penalty where block-level variance in single-agent baseline skill can trigger a FAIL even when L beats T under sensitivity and L beats S under primary groups. [If not required:] Only permits a dubious PASS if text T actively harmed Agent B relative to silence S within a specific macro block [...]. (That edge case is easily closed by requiring the point estimate mean(Brier_S − Brier_T) ≥ 0 or keeping Gate 2 at the primary group level.)"

**GPT-6 Astra Pro:** "Yes. Require both gates to have intervals entirely above zero under both the primary and sensitivity resampling. [...] For Gate 2, the failure shape is PASS without dependence-robust evidence that the latent system improves on silence. Beating text does not establish that separate requirement: text itself could be worse than the single receiver. [...] Requiring sensitivity survival can withhold PASS from a real benefit when the broader partition leaves inadequate precision. The correct interpretation is 'not established under the registered sensitivity assumption,' consistent with E4's operational FAIL definition."
