## E1 — Why the secondary endpoint exists: the claim and the motor-car rule (scoring rule v0.3 §1 and §10; `intent.md`)
> **§1. The claim this rule scores.** Given the same information, do agents communicating in latent states forecast real resolved events better than the same agents communicating in text, and is the gain concentrated where the two disagree?
> **§10. What this rule does not reward.** Being different from the crowd. Being different from text. Beating the crowd. Being confident. Any of these alone is "horse territory" by the motor-car tests.
> (`intent.md`) The motor-car question is whether useful divergent thinking is real: insights that exist in the geometry of the data but have no clean linguistic description. Performance alone — "our agents forecast better" — is horse territory.

## E2 — Scoring rule CANDIDATE v0.3, §7, the divergence paragraphs as written (committed `a6c2de8`)
> **Divergence:** a market is "divergent" if |p_L − p_T| ≥ delta. delta is pre-registered. Proposed 0.10; unaddressed by any cold round; still open.
> **Useful divergence (secondary endpoint):** on divergent markets only, aggregated to groups by the same means and weights: mean of (Brier_T − Brier_L) with a group-bootstrap interval, and the share of divergent markets where L is closer to the outcome than T. Both reported.
> (§11, open question 1) delta = 0.10: right line? scale with the base rate? (Carried from v0.1; no cold round has addressed it.)

## E3 — Cold round 5, Q6: the three answers, verbatim excerpts (committed `a6c2de8`)
**Grok 4.7:** "No evidence in briefing establishes that 0.10 is the right line in probability units, nor that it should or should not scale with base rate or distance from 0.5. The divergence secondary endpoint should be dropped as not pre-registrable; three cold rounds left it unaddressed, and nothing in the blocks supplies a falsifiable threshold or a power argument for any particular delta. If retained, it becomes falsifiable only if the secondary claim is pre-registered as 'the mean d_g on the divergent subset exceeds m with a group-bootstrap interval entirely above m,' with the same gates and sensitivity requirement as the primary endpoint."

**Gemini 3.1 Pro:** "Keep the secondary endpoint as descriptive only, OR replace fixed delta = 0.10 with a scale-aware threshold (specifically log-odds difference |logit(p_L) − logit(p_T)| ≥ delta_logit or scaled by √(p̄(1−p̄))), because a flat delta = 0.10 is distorted by the 80.2% NO base rate. [...] In a pool where 329 of 410 markets resolve NO (19.8% YES rate) and many markets are mutually exclusive slices, most well-calibrated probabilities sit near the boundaries (e.g., 0.02 to 0.15). A shift from p_T = 0.02 to p_L = 0.11 is a 5.5× change in odds [sic: the probability ratio; the odds ratio is about 6.1], yet fails delta = 0.10 (|0.11 − 0.02| = 0.09 < 0.10), whereas a shift from 0.45 to 0.55 near maximum uncertainty is a tiny informational update that passes delta = 0.10. Scaling the threshold by baseline uncertainty — e.g., defining divergence as |p_L − p_T| ÷ √(p̄(1−p̄)) ≥ 0.25 (where p̄ = (p_L + p_T)/2, which equals 0.125 at p̄ = 0.50 and 0.075 at p̄ = 0.10) — puts boundary markets and toss-up markets on the same statistical footing. [...] Because the set of markets satisfying |p_L − p_T| ≥ delta is a post-treatment conditioning set (determined by the models' outputs, which can have size N = 0 or N = 5), a bootstrap interval conditioned on a post-treatment subset cannot be a formal pass/fail gate without risking post-selection bias or division by zero. To make it falsifiable, pre-register a directional test on the full unconditional sample: the covariance (or regression slope) between magnitude of divergence |p_L − p_T| and paired Brier improvement d_m = Brier_T(m) − Brier_L(m) across all groups must be positive (Cov_g(|p_L − p_T|, d_g) > 0), testing whether larger latent-vs-text disagreements systematically produce larger accuracy gains without conditioning on an arbitrary cutoff delta."

**GPT-6 Astra Pro:** "Keep delta = 0.10 as a fixed, symmetric operational threshold: ten percentage points of probability, such as 0.30 versus 0.40. There is no evidence in briefing that this is an optimal cutoff, that it will select enough observations, or that E5's accuracy effects justify it. It is an interpretable design choice that must be fixed before forecasts. I would not scale it by the group's realized YES rate. That would allow outcomes to influence which forecasts enter the selected subset, while changing the question from absolute disagreement to a relative measure. [...] I would not scale it by distance from 0.5 either. Brier scoring does not give 0.5 a special decision role here. Such scaling would require another choice about whose forecast defines the distance and could make very small changes near 0.5 qualify as substantial divergence. [...] The endpoint can be pre-registered, but E4 currently specifies the selection rule without a complete secondary statistic or success criterion. I would add: fixed aggregation: within each event, average T-minus-L Brier differences over its divergent markets; average eligible event means within each primary group; then average equally across primary groups containing divergent markets. A fixed claim: for example, positive mean Brier improvement on divergent forecasts, established only if the interval lies above zero under both primary and sensitivity resampling. A support rule specifying when there are enough eligible groups and sensitivity blocks to estimate that conditional effect. No evidence in briefing supplies a justified numerical minimum. Explicit handling of no divergence or inadequate support: 'secondary claim not established,' without changing delta or treating the undefined effect as zero. Reporting of the fraction selected, its distribution across groups and blocks, and the size and uncertainty of the effect. No ability for this secondary result to rescue a primary FAIL. [...] A positive result on divergent forecasts would establish the specified conditional claim. It would not establish that the overall gain is concentrated there. That stronger claim requires a pre-registered comparison with nondivergent forecasts or a decomposition of the primary gain using its original weights."

## E5 — The primary endpoint and gates as now decided (cold round 6, November 3, 4 of 4; committed `701fe1d`, `81e4006`)
> Primary endpoint: paired difference per group, d_g = Brier_T(g) − Brier_L(g); mean over groups with a 95% bootstrap interval resampling primary groups; recomputed with the same weights resampling sensitivity blocks. PASS requires the interval entirely above the pre-registered margin m under both resamplings. Gate 1 (L beats the deranged control D) and Gate 2 (L beats the single agent S) each count as met only if the 95% interval lies entirely above zero under both resamplings. FAIL means "did not establish the required advantage," not "the advantage is absent." A secondary result can never rescue a primary FAIL.
