Technique: Sparse Distributed Representations (SDR) with Semantic Overlap Metric
Field: Computational Neuroscience (Numenta HTM theory)
Core idea: Encode latent deltas as fixed-sparsity binary vectors where semantic similarity is preserved by the degree of bit overlap, not Euclidean distance.
Why LatentForge: Your current top-k sparsity selects the largest-magnitude components, but this is magnitude-driven, not semantics-driven. HTM cortical columns use SDRs where two vectors that share ~2% of active bits are considered semantically related, and zero shared bits means completely unrelated — with formal noise tolerance guarantees. Applied to latent deltas: instead of transmitting the raw float values of top-128 components, you encode which *semantic subspaces* are active as a binary mask, then transmit only the residual float correction on top of a shared codebook. The overlap metric between two agents' SDRs gives you divergence signal essentially for free — the Shadow Self layer could compute semantic drift as `1 - (|A ∩ B| / |A ∪ B|)` on the binary masks alone, without ever decoding the floats. This decouples the governance signal from the payload, which is architecturally cleaner and dramatically cheaper.

SUGGESTION 2
Technique: Genomic Delta Encoding via Consensus Reference + VCF-style Variant Calls
Field: Computational Genomics (variant call format, population genomics)
Core idea: Store only the positions and magnitudes where an individual's genome *differs* from a population consensus — transmit "variants," not full sequences.
Why LatentForge: Your shared seed vector is already functioning as a genomic reference genome. What LatentForge is not yet doing is what genomics formalized in the VCF (Variant Call Format) standard: a strict separation between (a) the reference, (b) the position of divergence, (c) the type of change (substitution vs. insertion), and (d) a quality/confidence score per variant. Applied directly: latent deltas become "variant calls" — sparse records of the form `(dimension_index, delta_magnitude, confidence)`. The confidence score maps naturally onto your Shadow Self governance: low-confidence high-magnitude variants are flagged for review exactly as rare genomic variants with low read-depth are flagged in clinical pipelines. More powerfully, population genomics uses *linkage disequilibrium* — the fact that certain positions co-vary together — to compress variant sets by transmitting haplotype blocks instead of individual SNPs. In latent space, dimensions that co-activate can be grouped into "latent haplotypes," and you transmit a block ID + small residual instead of all component values. This is a direct path to sub-128 effective transmission cost with no information loss within the learned blocks.
Test in one week: On a MacBook, run PCA or sparse PCA (scikit-learn) on a matrix of 200+ latent-proxy vectors (Claude API logit distributions across diverse prompts). Identify the top 20 principal components as "haplotype blocks." Encode each vector as (block_weights, residual). Measure reconstruction error vs. compression ratio and compare to naive top-k. Target: same reconstruction error at 30% fewer transmitted floats.

SUGGESTION 3
Technique: Riemannian Exponential Map Compression via Tangent Space Linearization
Field: Differential Geometry / Geometric Statistics
Core idea: On a curved manifold, the shortest path between two points is a geodesic — map both points into the flat tangent space at a shared reference, compute the linear difference there, and transmit only that flat vector.
Why LatentForge: Latent spaces of large language models are not flat — they have curvature, and Euclidean deltas between hidden states are therefore geometrically distorted approximations of what actually changed semantically. The exponential map `exp_p(v)` and its inverse `log_p(q)` let you project any latent vector `q` into the tangent space at your shared seed point `p`, yielding a vector `v` that is a *locally flat, lossless linearization* of the curved-space difference. This is precisely what LatentForge needs: a compression basis that respects the geometry of the space rather than fighting it. The critical practical insight is that tangent space vectors near the seed point are *more compressible* by standard methods (top-k, PCA) because the curvature-induced redundancy has been factored out. Your seed vector stops being just a numerical reference and becomes a proper geometric basepoint, which also gives the Shadow Self layer a principled notion of "geodesic distance" for drift monitoring — not Euclidean distance, which is meaningless on a curved manifold.
