SUGGESTION 1
Technique: Sparse Distributed Representations (SDR) with Semantic Overlap Preservation
Field: Computational Neuroscience (Numenta / HTM Theory)
Core idea: Encode latent deltas as fixed-sparsity binary vectors where semantic similarity is preserved by the degree of bit-overlap between representations, mirroring how the neocortex encodes information in sparse, high-dimensional patterns.
Why LatentForge: Your current top-k sparsity already selects the k largest activations, but it treats each delta independently — it has no memory of which dimensions *tend* to co-activate. SDR theory suggests that if you learn which dimensions form stable "mini-columns" (dimensions that reliably fire together across messages), you can re-index your top-k selection around these columns rather than individual scalars. This means two deltas that are semantically close will naturally share more active columns, making overlap a cheap semantic distance metric the Shadow Self layer can exploit without recomputing full cosine similarity. The shared seed vector maps cleanly onto HTM's concept of a "union memory" — a stable background against which sparse deviations are measured. The key compression gain: instead of transmitting 128 raw float indices + values, you transmit 128-bit column IDs (learned once, shared), dramatically reducing index overhead.

SUGGESTION 2
Technique: Genomic Differential Compression via Run-Length + Positional Anchoring (inspired by VCF/BAM delta encoding)
Field: Computational Genomics (variant calling pipelines)
Core idea: Represent latent deltas not as floating-point vectors but as *variant records* against a reference genome (the shared seed), encoding only position, sign, and magnitude-bucket — identical to how VCF files store SNPs against a reference assembly.
Why LatentForge: Genomic pipelines solved almost exactly your problem: a reference sequence (your seed vector) is shared between parties, and individual genomes (agent states) differ from it in sparse, structured ways. VCF encoding stores only (chromosome, position, ref_allele, alt_allele) — no full sequence transmitted. Translated to LatentForge: each latent delta becomes a list of (dimension_index, sign_bit, quantized_magnitude) tuples. The key biological insight genomics adds beyond naive sparsity is *linkage disequilibrium* — nearby variant positions are correlated, so you can encode them as haplotype blocks rather than independent SNPs. In your latent space, dimensions with high mutual information can be grouped into "haplotype blocks" learned offline, and an entire block's delta can be predicted from one anchor dimension, transmitting only the residual. This is structurally different from standard sparse coding and has not appeared in any latent agent communication paper the field has produced.

Compute a pairwise MI matrix across dimensions. Use a greedy block-merging algorithm (20 lines of Python) to form 16 haplotype-style blocks. Encode deltas as: per-block anchor value + residuals, compare compression ratio and reconstruction MSE to flat top-128 sparsity. MacBook + numpy only.

SUGGESTION 3
Technique: Riemannian Centroid Tracking for Drift-Aware Delta Quantization
Field: Differential Geometry / Riemannian Statistics (Fréchet mean on manifolds)
Core idea: Instead of quantizing latent deltas in flat Euclidean space, maintain a running Fréchet mean (the geometric centroid on the latent manifold) as a second-order reference point, and quantize each new delta relative to its geodesic distance from this centroid rather than from the fixed seed.
Why LatentForge: Your Shadow Self layer monitors drift, but your compression step is currently geometry-blind — it quantizes in flat space even though high-dimensional latent representations live on curved manifolds. The Fréchet mean is the manifold analogue of the arithmetic mean: it's the point that minimizes the sum of squared geodesic distances to all past states. Using it as a *dynamic* compression reference (alongside the static seed) means your quantization grid is always centered where the agent's state actually lives, not where it started. This directly reduces the magnitude of deltas you need to transmit (smaller numbers → fewer bits), while the difference between the Fréchet mean and the seed vector gives the Shadow Self a principled, geometry-aware drift signal — not just cosine similarity, but actual manifold displacement. In practice, for PCA-reduced latent proxies, geodesic distance approximates well with second-order Taylor correction, making this tractable on CPU.

Implement Fréchet mean estimation via iterative Riemannian gradient descent on the PCA-projected manifold (geomstats library, pure Python, runs on MacBook). Compare: (a) delta magnitudes relative to fixed seed vs. (b) delta magnitudes relative to rolling Fréchet mean.
