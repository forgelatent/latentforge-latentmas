SUGGESTION 1
Technique: Sparse Distributed Representations (SDR) with Semantic Overlap Preservation
Field: Computational Neuroscience (Hierarchical Temporal Memory / Numenta)
Core idea: Encode latent deltas as fixed-sparsity binary vectors where semantic similarity is preserved by the degree of bit-overlap between representations, mirroring how the neocortex encodes information in sparse, overlap-structured firing patterns.
Why LatentForge: Your current top-k sparsity selects the k largest-magnitude dimensions, but discards the geometric relationship between which dimensions are active. SDR encoding reframes the delta as a binary mask where the *identity* of the active indices carries the semantic signal — not just their values. Two deltas that activate overlapping index sets are semantically related; orthogonal index sets signal true divergence. This means your Shadow Self layer could monitor drift using simple Hamming distance on the binary mask rather than expensive cosine similarity on dense vectors, and the shared seed vector becomes a canonical SDR against which all agent deltas are measured as set-theoretic differences. The fixed sparsity (e.g., exactly 2% of bits active) gives you a mathematically guaranteed noise-rejection property: random perturbations almost never land in the valid SDR subspace, so corrupted transmissions are detectable without checksums.

If Jaccard tracks cosine at r > 0.7, SDR overlap is a viable cheap divergence proxy. Pure Python + numpy, ~2 hours.

SUGGESTION 2
Technique: Wavelet Packet Decomposition with Adaptive Subband Selection
Field: Signal Processing / Cardiac Electrophysiology (ECG compression standards)
Core idea: Decompose the latent delta vector into a multi-resolution wavelet basis and transmit only the subbands whose energy exceeds a per-agent adaptive threshold, mirroring how ECG compression standards (AZTEC, SAPA) exploit the fact that physiological signals concentrate meaning in specific frequency bands that vary by context.
Why LatentForge: Latent deltas are not random — they have internal correlation structure that top-k scalar selection ignores entirely. A transformer's residual stream has coarse "topic-level" variation (low-frequency components of the delta) and fine "token-specific" variation (high-frequency components). Wavelet packet decomposition exposes this structure explicitly: you can transmit only the low-frequency subbands when agents are well-synchronized (high fidelity, low bitrate), and add high-frequency subbands selectively when divergence is detected. This is adaptive compression keyed to the semantic state of the conversation — something no current latent MAS system does. The shared seed vector defines the wavelet decomposition baseline; deltas are transmitted as coefficient residuals relative to that baseline, and the Shadow Self monitors which subbands are growing as an early-warning divergence signal.

Measure what fraction of total L2 energy is captured by the top-2 subbands across 100 random vectors drawn from a Gaussian with the correlation structure of a typical embedding (use a random covariance matrix). Compare reconstruction error of "top-2 subbands only" vs. "top-128 scalar values." PyWavelets is pip-installable, no GPU needed.

SUGGESTION 3
Technique: Fisher Information Matrix Diagonal as Compression Mask
Field: Statistical Mechanics / Evolutionary Biology (neutral theory of molecular evolution)
Core idea: Weight each latent dimension by its Fisher information — the sensitivity of the model's output distribution to perturbation in that dimension — and compress by transmitting only high-Fisher dimensions, analogous to how Kimura's neutral theory identifies which genomic positions are "selectively visible" vs. neutrally drifting.
Why LatentForge: The core problem with top-k magnitude selection is that a large-magnitude delta component may be semantically inert (the model's output is insensitive to it), while a small-magnitude component in a high-curvature region of the loss landscape may be critical. Fisher information diagonal estimates exactly this sensitivity: `F_ii ≈ E[(∂ log p(y|z)/∂z_i)²]`. Transmitting the top-k by Fisher score rather than magnitude gives you an *information-theoretically justified* compression mask — you are transmitting the dimensions that most change what the agent will do next, not just the dimensions that happen to be large. This maps directly to Kimura's insight that most genomic mutations are neutral (large but invisible to selection) while a few sites are under intense purifying selection (small variance, high information). The Shadow Self layer becomes a "purifying selection" operator: it can flag when transmitted dimensions shift from high-Fisher to low-Fisher regions, indicating the agent is drifting into neutral semantic space.

Compare overlap between top-128 by magnitude vs. top-128 by Fisher score. High disagreement = strong evidence for Fisher-based masking. Implementable with tiktoken + API calls + scipy, ~half a day.
