SUGGESTION 1
Technique: Sparse Distributed Representations (SDR) with fixed sparsity ratio
Field: Computational Neuroscience (Numenta / HTM Theory)
Core idea: Encode latent deltas as binary sparse vectors where exactly k bits are active, exploiting the combinatorial capacity of high-dimensional sparse codes to represent exponentially many states with minimal overlap.
Why LatentForge: Your current top-k sparsity is already structurally adjacent to SDRs, but HTM theory gives you something you're missing: a *principled capacity bound* and an *overlap-based similarity metric* for free. In biological cortex, two SDRs with <k/2 overlapping bits are treated as distinct memories — this directly maps to your divergence-preservation requirement. If you encode each latent delta as a fixed-sparsity binary vector (not just top-k floats, but binarized top-k indices), you gain: (1) lossless index transmission instead of float transmission, (2) set-intersection as a O(k) divergence proxy instead of cosine distance, and (3) natural protection against drift — the Shadow Self layer could monitor overlap-ratio decay between consecutive SDRs as a governance signal, rather than needing to reconstruct full vectors. The shared seed vector becomes the "union" background against which deltas are XOR-masked. This reframes your compression problem as a *combinatorial* rather than *geometric* one, which is both cheaper to compute and more theoretically tractable.

Binarize each via top-128 index selection. Measure: (a) overlap ratio between semantically similar vs. dissimilar pairs, (b) whether overlap rank-orders match cosine similarity rank-orders. If Spearman ρ > 0.85, your top-k is already SDR-compatible and you can drop float storage entirely for the index layer.

SUGGESTION 2
Technique: Predictive Coding with Precision-Weighted Residuals
Field: Theoretical Neuroscience (Friston / Active Inference)
Core idea: Transmit only the *prediction error* between what a receiving agent expects the sender's latent state to be and what it actually is, weighted by the inverse variance (precision) of each dimension — suppressing low-information, high-noise dimensions automatically.
Why LatentForge: Current LatentForge sends deltas relative to a shared seed, which is a first-order predictive coding move. But biological brains do *second-order* predictive coding: they also model the *reliability* of each dimension and weight transmission accordingly. Concretely, if both agents maintain a running estimate of per-dimension variance across recent latent vectors, the sender can transmit only dimensions where the precision-weighted prediction error exceeds a threshold — not a fixed top-k, but an *adaptive-k* determined by information content. This is strictly more efficient than top-k: in stable reasoning phases, k collapses to near zero (agents are in sync); in novel/divergent phases, k expands to capture genuine surprise. The Shadow Self governance layer maps perfectly onto the "precision estimator" role in active inference — it's already monitoring drift, so it can dynamically set the transmission threshold. This gives you domain-adaptivity (your stated target) without any learned parameters.
Test in one week: Using the Anthropic API, generate embedding sequences for 5 conversation threads (20 turns each). Compute per-dimension rolling variance across turns. At each turn, compare: (a) top-128 fixed sparsity reconstruction error vs. (b) precision-thresholded variable-k reconstruction error (threshold = mean + 1σ of precision-weighted error). Measure whether (b) achieves equal or lower reconstruction error at lower mean k. Expect k to drop 30–60% during topically stable turns.

SUGGESTION 3
Technique: Genomic Copy Number Variation (CNV) Differential Encoding
Field: Genomics / Comparative Genomics
Core idea: Rather than encoding absolute latent positions or even first-order deltas, encode *which regions of the latent space have amplified or deleted* relative to a reference genome (seed vector), using run-length compressed segment boundaries instead of per-dimension values.
Why LatentForge: In genomics, two organisms aren't compared dimension-by-dimension — instead, structural variants (duplications, deletions, inversions of contiguous segments) are detected and transmitted as *boundary coordinates* of changed regions. Your latent vectors have an analogous structure: meaningful changes tend to be *regionally coherent* in latent space (dimensions 40–55 all activate together for syntactic structure, dimensions 200–230 for emotional valence, etc.). If you sort dimensions by activation covariance cluster (a one-time offline step), contiguous runs of significant delta values will correspond to semantic features — and you can encode the delta as a list of (start_index, end_index, scale_factor) triples rather than 128 individual (index, value) pairs. For k=128 active dimensions that cluster into ~15 coherent regions, this reduces transmission to ~15 triples vs. 128 floats — roughly 8x additional compression on top of your existing 24x. The seed vector is literally your "reference genome." Drift monitoring becomes checking for unexpected new CNV regions, which is structurally identical to somatic mutation detection.
Test in one week: Embed 500 prompts via Anthropic API. Compute pairwise dimension covariance matrix. Apply hierarchical clustering (scipy, no GPU) to find natural dimension groupings. For 50 held-out delta pairs, compare bit cost of (a) top-128 sparse encoding vs. (b) CNV-style run-length encoding over clustered dimensions. Measure compression ratio and whether cosine similarity of decoded vectors is preserved within 2% of original. The clustering step is the entire experiment — if clusters are tight (intra-cluster variance < 20% of inter-cluster variance), CNV encoding is viable.
